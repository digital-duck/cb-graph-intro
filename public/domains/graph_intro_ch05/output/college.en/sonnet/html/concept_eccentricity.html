Eccentricity

Every conic section — circle, ellipse, parabola, hyperbola — can be described by a single number that measures how "stretched" or "open" it is. That number is eccentricity, denoted $e$, and it is defined as the ratio of two distances built into the curve's geometry:

$$e = \frac{c}{a}$$

where $a$ is the length of the semi-major axis and $c$ is the distance from the center to a focus. For a circle, the two foci collapse to the center, so $c = 0$ and $e = 0$. As the shape stretches into an elongated ellipse, $c$ grows toward $a$, and $e$ approaches 1. A parabola sits exactly at $e = 1$, and a hyperbola has $e > 1$ because its foci lie outside the curve entirely.

| Conic | Eccentricity | |---|---| | Circle | $e = 0$ | | Ellipse | $0 < e < 1$ | | Parabola | $e = 1$ | | Hyperbola | $e > 1$ |

Worked example. Earth's orbit is an ellipse with semi-major axis $a = 1.496 \times 10^8\ \text{km}$ and eccentricity $e \approx 0.0167$. Find the distance from Earth's center to the Sun (a focus).

From $e = c/a$:

$$c = e \cdot a = 0.0167 \times 1.496 \times 10^8 \approx 2.50 \times 10^6\ \text{km}$$

So the Sun sits about 2.5 million km off-center — close enough to nearly circular, which is why seasonal temperature differences are driven by axial tilt rather than orbital distance.

Problem-solving application. Suppose a satellite is placed in an elliptical orbit with perigee (closest point) $r_p = 7{,}000\ \text{km}$ and apogee (farthest point) $r_a = 42{,}000\ \text{km}$ from Earth's center. Find the eccentricity.

The semi-major axis and focal offset are:

$$a = \frac{r_a + r_p}{2} = \frac{42{,}000 + 7{,}000}{2} = 24{,}500\ \text{km}$$ $$c = \frac{r_a - r_p}{2} = \frac{42{,}000 - 7{,}000}{2} = 17{,}500\ \text{km}$$ $$e = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{17{,}500}{24{,}500} \approx 0.714$$

That high eccentricity means the satellite spends most of its time near apogee — a property deliberately exploited in Molniya orbits to provide long dwell time over high-latitude regions. Eccentricity, then, is not just a geometric label; it directly predicts physical behavior.
