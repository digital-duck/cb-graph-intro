Facility Placement

Where you put something matters as much as what it does. Facility placement is the problem of choosing locations for one or more service points — hospitals, warehouses, cell towers, fire stations — to optimize some measure of coverage, cost, or response time across a set of demand points. It sits at the intersection of logistics, operations research, and graph theory, and it shows up any time resources are finite and geography is real.

The core idea is straightforward: given a set of candidate sites and a set of customers (or demand points), select $k$ sites to open such that the total or maximum distance customers must travel is minimized. Two classic formulations capture most real-world variants:

  • $p$-median: minimize the total weighted distance from every demand point to its nearest open facility.
  • $p$-center: minimize the maximum distance any single demand point must travel — useful when equity or worst-case response time matters more than average efficiency.

    • Worked example. Suppose a county has five towns with populations $[800, 600, 1200, 400, 900]$ and you must place two clinics. Represent the towns as nodes in a graph with road distances as edge weights. For each pair of candidate sites, compute the assignment cost: each town is served by whichever clinic is closer, and the total cost is $\sum_i w_i \cdot d(i, \text{nearest clinic})$, where $w_i$ is the population and $d$ is road distance. Enumerate all $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ pairs, compute costs, and pick the minimum.

    import itertools

towns = ['A','B','C','D','E']
pop   = {'A':800,'B':600,'C':1200,'D':400,'E':900}
dist  = {('A','B'):10,('A','C'):20,('A','D'):15,('A','E'):25,
         ('B','C'):12,('B','D'):18,('B','E'):30,
         ('C','D'):8, ('C','E'):10,('D','E'):22}

def road(u, v):
    return dist.get((u,v), dist.get((v,u), 0 if u==v else float('inf')))

best, best_pair = float('inf'), None
for s1, s2 in itertools.combinations(towns, 2):
    cost = sum(pop[t] * min(road(t,s1), road(t,s2)) for t in towns)
    if cost < best:
        best, best_pair = cost, (s1, s2)

print(best_pair, best)

    Applying the concept. Real instances are too large for brute-force enumeration — a network with 500 candidate sites and $k=10$ yields over $10^{18}$ combinations. Practitioners use greedy interchange heuristics (open the single best site, then swap sites one at a time as long as cost drops) or integer linear programming solvers. When demand is uncertain — a storm may shift where ambulances are needed — stochastic extensions add scenario weights. The choice between $p$-median and $p$-center also carries an ethical dimension: optimizing average distance can leave remote communities with very long travel times, while $p$-center explicitly protects the worst-served location.
