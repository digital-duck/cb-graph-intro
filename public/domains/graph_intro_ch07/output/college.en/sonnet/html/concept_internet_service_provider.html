Internet Service Provider

An Internet Service Provider (ISP) is a company that sells access to the global internet infrastructure. When your device sends or receives data, it travels through your ISP's network before reaching the wider internet. ISPs manage the physical and logical connections — fiber-optic cables, wireless towers, routing equipment — that make this possible. They assign your device an IP address, route your traffic, and enforce the terms of your service plan, including speed caps and data limits.

ISPs operate at different scales. A residential ISP (Comcast, AT&T, Spectrum) connects homes and small businesses. A transit ISP or Tier-1 provider (Lumen, Telia) owns intercontinental backbone infrastructure and sells capacity to smaller ISPs. When you stream a video, your data may pass through two or three of these layers before arriving at your router.

Worked example. Suppose you type https://example.com into a browser. Your device sends a DNS query asking "what is the IP address of example.com?" That query travels first to your ISP's DNS resolver, which either answers from cache or forwards the query up the hierarchy. Once your browser has the IP address, it opens a TCP connection. Every packet you exchange with the server is routed by your ISP through its own backbone and handed off to upstream providers until it reaches example.com's data center. The reverse path brings the response back. Your ISP is the mandatory on-ramp and off-ramp for every step.

Problem-solving application. Network engineers use this model to diagnose connectivity failures systematically. If ping 8.8.8.8 (Google's DNS) succeeds but ping example.com fails, the problem is DNS resolution, not the ISP connection itself. If ping 8.8.8.8 also fails but your router's admin page loads, the fault lies at or beyond the ISP handoff point — a downed line, misconfigured gateway, or ISP outage. Working outward from the local device toward the backbone isolates the broken layer. Understanding where your ISP fits in the path turns a frustrating "internet is down" complaint into a testable, layer-by-layer diagnosis.
