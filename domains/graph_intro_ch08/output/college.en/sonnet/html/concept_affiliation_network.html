Affiliation Network

Definition

An affiliation network (also called a two-mode or bipartite network) records which actors belong to which groups or events. There are two kinds of nodes, such as people and clubs, authors and papers, or actors and movies. Edges connect only nodes of different kinds. A person links to a club, but a person never links directly to another person.

The data are stored in an incidence matrix $B$, with one row per actor and one column per group:

$$B_{ij} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if actor } i \text{ belongs to group } j \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

Affiliation data lets us infer relationships that were never observed directly. Two actors who share a group are indirectly connected. Two groups that share members are also indirectly connected.

Worked example

Four students join three clubs:

| | Chess | Robotics | Band | |---|---|---|---| | Ana | 1 | 1 | 0 | | Ben | 1 | 0 | 0 | | Cai | 0 | 1 | 1 | | Dee | 0 | 1 | 1 |

To find how many clubs each pair of students shares, project onto the actors by computing $A = BB^{T}$. Entry $A_{ik}$ counts the groups that actors $i$ and $k$ have in common, and the diagonal $A_{ii}$ gives each actor's number of memberships.

  • Ana and Ben share Chess, so $A_{\text{Ana,Ben}} = 1$.
  • Ana and Cai share Robotics, so $A_{\text{Ana,Cai}} = 1$.
  • Cai and Dee share Robotics and Band, so $A_{\text{Cai,Dee}} = 2$.
  • Ben shares nothing with Cai or Dee, so those entries are $0$.

    • Projecting onto the groups uses $G = B^{T}B$. Here $G_{\text{Robotics,Band}} = 2$ because Cai and Dee belong to both clubs.

    Problem-solving application

    Problem. The school wants to spread a flyer through the students. Whom should it target, and which clubs act as bridges?

    Step 1: Find the most connected actors. Count each student's distinct contacts, meaning the nonzero off-diagonal entries in their row of $A$. Ana has two contacts (Ben and Cai), Cai has two (Ana and Dee), Ben has one, and Dee has one. Ana and Cai are the best seeds.

    Step 2: Find the bridging group. Robotics contains Ana, Cai, and Dee, so it has the most members. It is also the only club that connects Ana to the Cai–Dee pair. If Robotics were removed, Ana and Ben would be split off from Cai and Dee.

    Step 3: Interpret the projection with care. The projection loses information. In $A$, Cai–Dee has weight 2, which could mean two separate interactions or one shared membership counted twice. A club with 50 members also creates 1,225 student pairs, but those ties are probably weaker than the pair from a 3-person club. Analysts often normalize the weights, for example by dividing by group size, to correct for this.

    Takeaway. Keep the bipartite structure when group size matters. Project it only when you need a one-mode network.
